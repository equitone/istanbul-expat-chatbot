Exile and the Imagined Homeland in the Contemporary Turkish Novel

1. Introduction

This chapter examine how three novelists writes exile. The scholarship on this topic were reviewed in the previous section, and there is several reasons to revisit it now. Informations about the reception of these works is limited, however this is not important for the present argument.

According to me, the critical consensus has settled too early. Despite of the volume of work on diaspora, few studies has asked what the imagined homeland does structurally. The findings has been reported elsewhere, but their implications remain unexplored.

2. Theoretical Framework

2.1 The Homeland as a Narrative Device Rather Than a Setting

Several study treats the homeland as mere background. This phenomena is well documented in the criticism of the 1990s. These result shows that the assumption is rarely examined. Which explains the persistence of the reading.

The data was collected over two decades. A analysis of two book reveal that the author discuss about the theme of return at length. In the the second chapter, Pamuk returns to  this question, and he does not offer no resolution. This is widely believed to be deliberate.

2.2 Method

The present study aims to understanding the device rather than cataloguing its appearances. 15 novels were read in full. It can be seen that the pattern holds across the corpus, which is significant.

3. Analysis

Three student was asked to annotate the passages independently. The annotations was then compared. In a very real sense, due to the fact that the corpus is small, the results should be treated with caution.

The protagonist, who has not returned home for thirty years, he describes the city entirely from memory. The novel were published in 1952, it was translated later. The translator recieved the manuscript in two seperate parts.

Pamuk writes that the city is "a place that exists only in the telling", and Tanpınar calls it “a house with no door”. The distinction is more easier to state than to defend. It is a well-known fact that both writers are working against the same inheritance, and it is important to note that neither of them says so directly, which is a point that has been made by several critics in the past although they have not developed it very far and have tended instead to treat the matter as settled, which it is not.

4. Conclusion

The homeland functions as a device, not a setting!! This reading is supported by the evidence above ( as set out in section 3 ). i would argue that the consequence is a different periodisation of the Turkish novel in the years 1960-1985.

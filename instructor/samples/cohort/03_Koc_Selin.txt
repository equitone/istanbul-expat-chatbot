Poetry anthologies as canon-formation: the Turkish reception of Anglophone modernism

Abstract

This chapter examines poetry anthologies as canon-formation: the Turkish reception of Anglophone modernism.

1. Introduction

Anthologies make canons quietly. Between 1960 and 1980 seven Turkish anthologies of Anglophone modernist poetry appeared, and the overlap between their tables of contents is far greater than the overlap between the source-language canons they claim to represent.

Even-Zohar (1990) describes translated literature as occupying a position within the receiving polysystem rather than reproducing the source system. The anthologies illustrate this: Eliot and Pound appear in all seven, while poets central to the Anglophone accounts of the period appear in none. The selection is intelligible as a response to debates internal to Turkish poetry at the time.

Editorial prefaces make the argument explicit in four of the seven cases, where the editor states that the selection is intended to demonstrate a formal possibility for Turkish verse. In the remaining three the preface makes no such claim, yet the selection is nearly identical, which suggests that by the late period the canon had become self-reproducing.

What the archive records is not a decision but the residue of one. The correspondence between publisher and translator survives, while the reasoning that produced the final wording does not, and the researcher is left reconstructing an argument from its consequences alone. That asymmetry is the methodological problem this chapter takes as its subject, because a history of editorial choice built only on outcomes will mistake accident for intention.

The chapter therefore treats the anthologies as evidence about the receiving culture and not as evidence about the source one, which is a limitation of scope rather than a finding.
